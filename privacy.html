2. What this policy covers

It does not cover other websites, including Google, whose own privacy policy applies to your use of their services.

3. What personal data the application processes

The application is used to prepare and issue invoices, so most of what it holds is business records. Under the GDPR, a sole trader's name, address and VAT number are still personal data when they identify a person, so we treat the whole record as personal data and protect it accordingly.

Client and contact details. Company or trading name, address, contact person, email address, telephone number, VAT number (btw-id), Chamber of Commerce number, and any purchase order or reference number a client requires on its invoices.

Invoice data. Invoice number, invoice date, due date, a description of the work and the dates it was carried out, quantities and day rates, amounts excluding VAT, the VAT rate or the reverse-charge statement, the total, payment status, and any credit note or correction linked to the invoice.

Work schedule data. The dates, times, location and project name of shifts. This comes either from a schedule feed published by the client, or from the owner's own Google Calendar when the owner connects it.

Email content. The recipient address, subject line and text of the email that sends an invoice, held only until the email is sent.

Account data. One login email address and a password. The password is stored as a hash by the authentication provider; we never see it in readable form.

Documents. The invoice PDFs themselves, which repeat the client details and invoice data above.

The application does not ask for, and does not want, special categories of data (such as health data), and it is not used to build profiles of individuals.

4. Google user data: what is accessed and why

The application connects to the owner's own Google account, with the owner's permission, given on Google's consent screen. It requests access to three Google services, and only uses them for the purposes below.

Google Drive. To store invoice PDFs in a folder structure per client, so that an issued invoice can be found and attached to an email later. Access is limited to the folders and files the application creates; it does not open, read or index other files in the Drive.

Google Calendar. To read the owner's work schedules, so that shifts can be matched to a client and a project and turned into invoice lines without being retyped. When the owner chooses to add a work entry from the application, a matching calendar entry is created in the same calendar.

Gmail. To send the invoice email from the owner's own Gmail address, with the invoice PDF attached. The application sends mail; it does not read the mailbox, and it does not scan incoming messages.

The permission token that allows this access is stored on the server side, not in the browser, and can be revoked at any time by the owner from the Google Account permissions page or by disconnecting the account in the application. Revoking it stops all further access.

7. Legal basis for processing

Under article 6 of the GDPR:

  • Performance of a contract (art. 6(1)(b)) — processing client and invoice data to carry out work and issue an invoice for it.
  • Legal obligation (art. 6(1)(c)) — keeping invoices and business records for the period Dutch tax law requires (see section 10).
  • Legitimate interest (art. 6(1)(f)) — running and securing the business and the application: keeping the records accurate, preventing misuse, keeping the system working. Where this basis is used, the interest is the ordinary interest of a small business in keeping correct books, and it does not override the rights of the people concerned.
  • Consent (art. 6(1)(a)) — connecting a Google account, which is a choice and can be undone at any time. Withdrawing consent stops further access to that account; it does not affect processing that already happened.

9. How data is stored and protected

  • The application is not a public service. There is one account, registration is disabled, and access is restricted to the account owner. Sign-in is required for every page that shows data.
  • Data is encrypted in transit (TLS) between the browser, the application and the database.
  • Data at rest is encrypted by the hosting and database providers.
  • The database enforces access rules per account, so a request without a valid session returns nothing.
  • Invoice PDFs are held in private storage and in the owner's own Drive. They are not published, indexed or linked publicly.
  • The Google permission token is held server-side, not in the browser.

No system is perfect. We cannot promise that a breach will never happen, but the measures above are chosen so that the data is not reachable without the owner's credentials or a compromise of a provider.

10. How long data is kept

Invoices and business records: seven years. Dutch tax law requires a business to keep its administrative records, including invoices, for seven years (art. 52 AWR, the Algemene wet inzake rijksbelastingen). This is a legal obligation, so an invoice cannot be deleted on request before that period ends.

Work schedule data: kept while it is needed to prepare an invoice, and afterwards only as part of the invoice record (the dates worked and the project). Schedule entries that never became an invoice are removed when they are no longer needed.

Email drafts and content: deleted once the invoice email has been sent. The sent message itself then sits in the owner's own Gmail account, under Google's retention rules and the owner's control.

Account data: kept while the account exists. If the application is discontinued, the account and its data are deleted, except for records that must be kept under the seven-year rule.

Google tokens: kept until the Google account is disconnected or the permission is revoked.

11. Your rights

If you are in the European Union, you have the right to:

  • access the personal data held about you, and receive a copy (art. 15 GDPR);
  • correct data that is wrong or incomplete (art. 16);
  • delete data, where no legal obligation requires it to be kept (art. 17);
  • restrict processing while a question about the data is being resolved (art. 18);
  • object to processing based on legitimate interest (art. 21);
  • receive your data in a portable format, where the processing is based on consent or contract and is carried out by automated means (art. 20);
  • withdraw consent at any time, where processing is based on consent (art. 7(3)).

How to exercise them. Send an email to Yordancreatives@gmail.com with the subject line "Data request", describing what you want. You do not need to use a form. We will respond within one month. If a request is complex or there are several, that period may be extended by two further months, and you will be told why. If we cannot be sure who is asking, we may ask for information to confirm identity — no more than is needed to answer the request.

There is no charge for a request, unless it is manifestly unfounded or excessive, in which case we may charge a reasonable fee or refuse, and will explain why.

About invoice data specifically. If you are a client and your details appear on an invoice, we may not be able to delete that invoice before the seven-year period ends, because keeping it is a legal obligation. In that case we will tell you what is held and why it cannot be removed, and delete anything that is not covered by the obligation.
