What the work actually is

Three kinds of job, all of them hands-on: something has to be mounted, cabled, checked, and taken down again without disrupting a shop floor or an event.

  • 01

    Winter lighting at retail locations

    Installing seasonal light installations in and around shops and shopping areas: mounting, cabling, checking every fixture, and removing the installation at the end of the season.

  • 02

    Event production

    Building and installing what an event needs physically built — stands, sets and display elements — set up before opening and struck afterwards.

  • 03

    Retail display work

    Installing displays and seasonal presentation in stores, working to a plan supplied by the retailer or the brand, and to their schedule rather than mine.

How a job runs

No account managers and no layers. You deal with the person who does the work.

  1. Brief and site check

    What has to go where, what the location allows, and when the work can happen — including outside opening hours.

  2. Quote

    A written quote for the job, excluding BTW, with what is included and what is not. Travel is stated separately rather than buried in the price.

  3. Installation and handover

    Materials, tools and transport are arranged; the installation is tested on site, walked through with whoever is responsible for the location, and cleaned up.

A second thing, still being built

Alongside the installation work I am building invoicing software for Dutch freelancers. It is the reason this site also has a privacy policy and terms: the tool asks for access to a Gmail account, and that access has to be explained honestly.

Invoicing for freelancers (ZZP)

In development

The problem is a familiar one. A freelancer finishes a job and then has to produce an invoice that satisfies the Belastingdienst — sequential numbering, the right VAT treatment, the required fields — and get it to the client without buying a full accounting package or re-typing everything into a template.

What the tool is being built to do:

  • Create invoices that meet the Dutch invoicing requirements, including a numbering series that stays sequential and unbroken.
  • Send the invoice from the freelancer's own email address, so it arrives from a known sender and not from a bulk-mail domain.
  • Keep the record of what was sent, in a form that can be handed to a bookkeeper.

It is not finished and it is not on the market. There are no customers, no pricing, and no sign-up — nothing here is an offer of service. When it is ready to be used by someone other than me, that will be stated plainly on this page first.
