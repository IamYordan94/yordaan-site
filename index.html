What we do

Physical work on site, and digital work at a desk. The physical side happens at night in a fixed window, so the street is back in use before opening. The digital side is built the same way: understand the job properly, then make the thing that does it.

  • 01

    On site — installation

    We install and take down winter lighting at shopping streets, shopping centres and public locations, and check every run of light before the season starts.

  • 02

    Production and build

    Not only fitting. We prepare, assemble and adapt the material before it goes up, and solve the problems that only appear on site — a bracket that does not fit the facade, a run of lights that has to cross a doorway.

  • 03

    Subcontracted crew work

    We work as a subcontractor for a main contractor, on their schedule and their site rules, alongside other crews. Where we are told, at the hour we are told, with the right tools.

  • 04

    Digital — software

    We design and build software: a scheduling tool for shift-based businesses, a stock-counting app, and the invoicing tool below. Built and maintained by the same person who does the site work.

  • 05

    Digital — automation

    The direction of travel: practical automation, increasingly with AI, for small businesses that have no technical team. The kind of work that quietly eats a day a week, made to run on its own. Taken on slowly, one job at a time.

Who we are

There is no team behind the name, and no premises besides the registered address: one person, who quotes the work, does the work and sends the invoice.

Two practices under one name. On site: mostly night work for a main contractor, invoiced per day per project, with VAT reverse-charged to the contractor (btw verlegd, article 12(1) of the Dutch VAT Act), plus design-and-build work — making the thing rather than only fitting it. At a desk: software and automation, including products of our own. The thread between them is the same: understand how a thing actually works, then make it work.

Quotes are in writing and per project. If the work changes on site, the change is agreed before it is carried out rather than presented afterwards.

The invoicing tool

A second, smaller thread: an invoicing tool for freelancers whose work arrives as shifts on a schedule. It is the reason this site has a privacy policy and terms at all.

Shift work in, invoice out

In development

The idea is simple — a finished shift should become an invoice line, not something retyped from a calendar at the end of the month. The tool reads a work schedule, lets the operator record the days worked, and produces the invoice as a PDF. It can store those PDFs in the operator's own Google Drive and email them from the operator's own Gmail account.

It is in development. There are no customers and nothing to sign up for. It runs on a single account, the owner's, with sign-ups switched off, and it exists because the work described above needed it. If that changes, it will be described here as something else.

The tool asks for access to Google Drive, Google Calendar and Gmail, and uses that access only to store invoice documents, read work schedules and send invoices. What it covers, and what it does not, is set out in the privacy policy.
